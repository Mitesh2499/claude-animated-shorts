## What it is (format B: "sage parable")
A warm, AI-illustrated Pixar-ish 2D cartoon. A bald, white-bearded sage with a Vaishnava tilak sits under a banyan in a purple galaxy (the frame story). He is the narrator AND an unseen presence who "was watching" three ordinary young people fail, then blesses each with a glowing hand on the forehead, and promises "I am behind you" at the end. Not 40 s: 111 s, because it tells three stories in parallel.

## The 7-point analysis
1. **Hook:** a direct question to the viewer ("Has it ever happened to you that you gave everything and no success?"), shown by a calm, kind face, not an event. It works because the viewer is the protagonist; stakes are the viewer's own feeling. Latency to first word: 1 s (black frame 0 only).
2. **Structure/pacing/transitions:** Frame story (sage) -> 3 parallel micro-stories (each ~14 s, same 4 beats: effort, passing time, proof of no result, giving up) -> proverb + metaphor -> tactics -> they retry -> time skip -> 3 payoffs -> sage again. Average shot ~2.2 s; dissolves of 0.1-0.4 s (ghost double-exposure) instead of hard cuts; black dips to separate chapters at 6, 12, 49, 52, 62, 87 s.
3. **Emotional triggers and relatability:** three universal struggles (body/gym, job rejection, exam), each with a single concrete symbol: scale reading 82.9 kg, "₹20 LPA" chat from a friend, "42/100" mock test. The comparison pain (friend's 20 LPA) is the sharpest. Characters are drawn warm and non-glamorous so viewers see themselves.
4. **Conversations/expressions/pauses:** nobody talks. The sage narrates; characters only act (look at the mirror, close the laptop, switch off the alarm). Each failure ends on a small defeated act (push shoes away, close laptop, switch off alarm) = giving up shown as a gesture.
5. **Suspense/curiosity loops/revelations:** loop 1: "who is watching?" (the refrain, 3x); loop 2: the boat riddle "will both arrive at the same speed?"; loop 3: the nakshatra pun is a mini-reveal; final: "what was actually found?" answered in the last 8 s ("in the first step itself").
6. **Cinematography/music/SFX:** vertical, mostly medium shots with a slow push; extreme close-ups of things (calendar, scale, phone, mock paper) as inserts (1-2 s); sage always in his cosmic purple/orange frame, characters in their rooms. Colour tells story: warm daylight (struggle) -> blue-purple night (despair + sage visit) -> orange dawn (new attempt) -> golden mirror/sunset (success). Golden glow on the sage's hand = blessing = the only supernatural effect.
7. **Ending/shareability:** a promise to the viewer ("I am behind you"), a proverb-type takeaway (patience = not dropping the oar), and a quotable reframe ("what you lost was found in the first step"). Share trigger: "send to someone who is about to quit".

## Technique inventory (reuse)
- **Burned-in captions** (unlike the Krishna sample): bottom, white bold sans in a rounded black box, 2-5 words per card, swap with the voice, mid-size (~7 % of height), some cards dim in black chapters. Marathi in Devanagari. A top caption "काही महिन्यांनंतर..." for time skips.
- **Refrain**: "(आणि) मी पाहत होतो" after every story; rhythmic, makes the sage present without exposition.
- **Triple parallel** with rule-of-three: same four beats, different symbols, then the same three again at the payoff (mirror of the setup).
- **Chapter dips**: black frame 0.3-1 s between chapters with a small grey caption.
- **Metaphor swap**: for the lesson, leave the characters and speak through a physical analogy (two boats, wind, waves, oar). Tactical advice is in the metaphor, never as a lecture.
- **Light grammar** as above.
- **Golden hand** on the forehead: one effect, repeated for each person.
- **Pun/wisdom beat** before the payoff (Punarvasu = "again light"): a cultural fact the audience may not know gives a "wow, I learnt something" moment and justifies a rewatch.
- **Time skip + montage** (1.9 s metronome) for payoffs: pace increases, music rises.

## WHY it works (principles)
1. The viewer is addressed first, in a question; the story that follows is proof that the viewer is not alone.
2. Three people = three audiences; each viewer sees himself/herself in one, so retention is high even at 111 s.
3. The repeated refrain turns the narrator into a presence, which makes the closing promise land as comfort, not preaching.
4. Advice is given as a physical metaphor (boats) so it is memorable and quotable, followed by action (they retry) rather than another speech.
5. The final reframe (the real win was the first step) gives a twist that makes success feel earned, not only lucky, and is the line viewers share.

## Weaknesses to improve (our version)
- 111 s is long for Reels; aim 60-75 s with TWO stories + a callback to a third, or keep three but 10 s each.
- The "success" payoff is all outcomes; add one concrete cost/sacrifice so it feels true.
- Several scenes repeat (gym, laptop); give each person one distinct visual motif (object that appears first and last).
- The sage never gets a visual reaction to the failures (only a standing pose): add a flinch, hand on heart, or a lamp he lights.
