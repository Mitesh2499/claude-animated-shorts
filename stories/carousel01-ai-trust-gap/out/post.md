# The AI Trust Gap - publishing package

## Caption

84% of developers are adopting AI coding tools, but only 33% trust what those tools write.

The Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025 (49,000+ devs) found that more developers now distrust AI accuracy (46%) than trust it. The #1 frustration: AI code that's "almost right, but not quite" (66%).

Then there's METR's controlled trial. Experienced open-source developers expected AI to make them 24% faster and felt 20% faster afterwards. Measured on 246 real tasks, they were 19% slower. It's a small study of early-2025 tools, but the gap between how fast AI feels and how fast it actually is should worry anyone who has stopped checking.

Slide 7 has 5 habits that close that gap. Save it for your next AI-assisted PR.

Honest answer in the comments: has AI made you FASTER, SLOWER or the SAME?

## Alt text (paste per slide: Advanced settings -> Write alt text)

1. Cover slide. Headline: 84% of developers are adopting AI, only 33% trust it. Two ring charts: a blue ring filled to 84% labelled 'use or plan to use AI tools' and an amber ring filled to 33% labelled 'trust the accuracy of its output'. Source: Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025.
2. Horizontal bar chart. 84% use or plan to use AI tools, 46% distrust the accuracy of AI output, 33% trust it, and only 3% highly trust it. Takeaway: adoption is near-universal, confidence is not.
3. Column chart comparing developers who distrust AI accuracy: 31% in 2024 versus 46% in 2025, an increase of 15 percentage points.
4. Big statistic: 66% of developers say AI solutions are 'almost right, but not quite', the top frustration. A second stat: 45% say debugging AI-generated code takes longer. Note: almost-right code looks finished, so it slips past review.
5. Column chart from the METR randomized trial with 16 experienced open-source developers and 246 tasks. Developers expected AI to make them 24% faster and felt 20% faster afterwards, but measured completion time was 19% slower, shown as a red bar below the zero line.
6. Three reasons for the slowdown reported in the METR trial: developers knew the code better than the AI, the repositories were large and complex, and time saved writing code was spent prompting, waiting and reviewing output. Caveat: a snapshot of early-2025 tools in one setting, not proof that AI slows everyone.
7. Checklist titled 5 habits that close the trust gap: ask for small diffs, write the test first, feed it real context such as files and error logs, read every line before you commit, and measure your speed with and without AI instead of guessing.
8. Closing slide asking: has AI made you faster, or does it just feel faster? Comment faster, slower or same. Buttons for comment, save and share, and a follow card for @MiteshJourney: evidence-based AI and dev breakdowns, no hype.
