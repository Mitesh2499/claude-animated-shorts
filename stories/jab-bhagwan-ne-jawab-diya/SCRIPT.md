# जब भगवान ने जवाब दिया... (rewrite v1, from the user's draft)

Pattern A (karma/divine reveal, adapted: the "wound" is the man's own exhaustion). Hindi. 38 s. ~84 spoken words (2.2 words/s). Captions optional (end card included as the user's draft had one).
Source of the idea: the user's own draft. Fact used: Bhagavad Gita 2.47 "कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन" (real verse, quoted exactly).

## What changed from the draft, and why
1. **Hook was text on black.** A card is not a wound. Now frame 1 is the man, a clay pot of bare soil, and 90 tally marks on the wall: the viewer sees "90 days of effort, nothing" in 1 s, with sound off.
2. **Generic complaint ("कितना इंतज़ार करूँ") -> one concrete symbol:** the unsprouted pot. Seed = struggle, no explanation needed.
3. **Dramatic-irony insert (new):** an extreme close-up underground shows the seed with one hair-thin white root. The audience knows what he cannot. This is the curiosity loop and plants the twist before the reveal.
4. **The stranger now calls him "पार्थ"** (his name is Kartik). Small mystery before the reveal ("how does he know...?") instead of a stranger simply lecturing.
5. **Krishna's lines cut from 5 to 3 sentences**, the Gita verse kept as the sacred anchor, and the 2.47 meaning told as "कर्म तेरा, फल मेरा" (simple, warm).
6. **One twist (new):** "हर सुबह जब तू पानी देता था, मैं इसी चौखट पर बैठा था" = the unanswered prayer had been heard every day. It turns complaint into companionship. This is the line viewers will send.
7. **Closing line shortened** to 10 words: "हर अंधेरा तोड़ने नहीं आता... कभी-कभी जड़ें देने आता है।"
8. Ordinary-voice vs divine-voice registers separated (Kartik plain Hindi; stranger/Krishna calm, shuddh, slow).

## Concept
- Logline: A man who has watered an empty pot for 90 days asks God for one answer; a stranger who calls him "Partha" gives it.
- Moral: What you cannot see is not what is not happening.
- Twist: Krishna was sitting at his doorstep every morning.
- Closing line: "हर अंधेरा तोड़ने नहीं आता... कभी-कभी जड़ें देने आता है।" (Har andhera todne nahin aata... kabhi-kabhi jadein dene aata hai.)
- Share trigger: "send to someone who is about to give up".
- Hashtags: #Krishna #BhagavadGita #Motivation #Karma #HindiReels

## Voice notes
- Kartik: young, hoarse, low, tired; no shouting; breaks on "जवाब".
- Stranger (before reveal): old, gentle, unhurried, a smile in the voice; nothing divine yet.
- Krishna: slow (~2 words/s), warm, resonant, half-beat pause before "जड़ों", "फल", "बैठा"; reverb tail on the last word.
