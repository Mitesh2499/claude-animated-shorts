# Pattern B: "Sage parable" (60-90 s, voice-over narrator, captions ON)

Learned from `Raw/Video-43709.mp4` (see `references/reel-script/VIDEO43709_ANALYSIS.md`). Use when the topic is a feeling the viewer has (quitting, comparison, patience, hope), not a villain-and-justice story (that is Pattern A, 30-45 s).

## Beat sheet (scale to 60-90 s; times for 75 s)
| time | beat | spoken-word budget | rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | HOOK QUESTION by the sage | 12-16 words | "Has it ever happened that ...? / कधी असं झालंय का ...?" End on the unfinished feeling. Sage face on a cosmic/banyan frame, first word within 1 s. |
| 5-30 | 2-3 PARALLEL STORIES, ~8-10 s each | 25-30 words each | Same 4 beats each: (1) effort, (2) time passes (calendar / season), (3) proof of no result (ONE concrete object: scale, mock paper, chat), (4) a defeated gesture (push away, close, switch off). Name + one detail each. |
| after each story | REFRAIN | 4-5 words | The sage "was watching" ("आणि मी हे सर्व पाहत होतो"). Repeat verbatim every time. |
| 30-40 | PROVERB + RIDDLE | 18-24 words | A two-line proverb (opposites), then a physical riddle: "Two boats left together. Will both arrive at the same time?" Then the foolish act: the one who flips his own boat because the other is ahead. |
| 40-50 | TACTICS IN METAPHOR + REFRAME | 25-30 words | Three conditional lines (opposite wind -> turn sail; big waves -> row carefully; no shore -> never drop the oar). Then the definition: "X is not waiting for the waves to stop. It is not letting go of the oar." Sage touches each sleeper's forehead, golden glow. |
| 50-55 | WISDOM / PUN BEAT | 15 words | A true cultural fact that reframes darkness as a new start (e.g. a nakshatra name, a proverb origin, a word root). Source it. |
| 55-60 | THEY TRY AGAIN | 12 words | Dawn light replaces night; each person does the SAME small action they had abandoned (shoes, apply, study mistakes). Parallel cuts. |
| 60-70 | TIME SKIP + 3 PAYOFFS | 18 words | "काही महिन्यांनंतर..." (top caption). 1.9 s metronome cuts. Each win is a symbol that mirrors the setup symbol (scale -> mirror smile; rejection mail -> offer mail; 42/100 -> name on list). |
| 70-75 | TWIST + PROMISE | 22-26 words | "Got what they wanted today. But what had been lost was found in the very first step." Then the sage: reassurance (an ending address to the viewer by name/by title) and "I am behind you". Fade to black. |

Total about 150-190 words at 1.7-2.2 words/s (Marathi/Hindi narration is slower than English).

## Language rules (Marathi / Hindi)
- Language = what the user asks, else match the pasted sample. Write Devanagari for every spoken line + Roman transliteration + one-line English gloss.
- Narrator (sage): warm, simple, spoken Marathi/Hindi, proverbs, "अहो" / "बघा" address, 3-7 word sentences, no heavy Sanskrit except the one wisdom beat.
- Ordinary characters speak little or nothing in this pattern; if they do, 1 short line each.
- Closing promise is second person plural-respect ("भिऊ नका", "मी तुमच्या पाठीशी आहे").
- Keep the proverb REAL and in common use. If unsure, say so in a note and offer an alternative.
- Never mock a faith; the sage is the moral centre.

## Captions (this pattern only)
White bold sans, rounded black box, bottom third, 2-5 words per card, swap on the voice; chapter dips use a small grey caption; time skips use a top caption. Keep our own channel tag, not the sample's.

## Visual hand-off (maps to illustrated-parallax-short)
- Frame story: sage portrait on a cosmic banyan background (reused for hook, mid-lecture close-up, closing).
- Per person: 6-7 layers (room, person, object, 2 faces, 1 insert, 1 mood-light variant night/dawn).
- Lighting states per room: day warm -> night purple-blue -> dawn orange -> success golden. Same room in each state = cheap to produce.
- Effects: ghost cross-dissolve 0.1-0.4 s, black dips, golden blessing glow on forehead, slow push 1-2 %/s, 1-2 s inserts of objects.

## Self-check
- [ ] Hook is a question to the viewer, sage on screen within 1 s
- [ ] Each story has exactly the 4 beats and ONE concrete symbol
- [ ] Refrain repeated word for word
- [ ] Proverb is real; wisdom fact is true and sourced
- [ ] Payoffs mirror the setups symbol-for-symbol
- [ ] The twist line says the real win was earlier than the result
- [ ] Ends with a promise to the viewer, not a moral lecture
